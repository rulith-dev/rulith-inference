Rulith Inference 0.8.0 adds a **Brain** page: Rulith Brain, long-term memory for your local model, runs inside Inference with the model you already have. It also fixes a failed allocation when a long conversation with an image comes back from the SSD cache ([#33](https://github.com/rulith-dev/rulith-inference/issues/33)), stops the app's status checks from producing failed-login lines ([#32](https://github.com/rulith-dev/rulith-inference/issues/32)) and makes sure only one engine can listen on a port ([#28](https://github.com/rulith-dev/rulith-inference/issues/28)).

**Brain inside Inference**
- A new **Brain** entry in the sidebar opens Rulith Brain in the app. The first visit downloads the Brain runtime (about 90 MB), checks its signature and installs it; models already in your Inference folders are found and used, nothing is downloaded twice.
- Inference and Brain take turns on the GPU: switching saves the side you leave, stops its engine and starts the other one. A conversation you leave in Inference continues where it was when you come back.
- Brain in Inference and the Rulith Brain app share one place for your licence, memories and settings: a memory learned on one side answers on the other.
- New users get the 30-day trial from the Brain page. Licences: [rulith.ai/brain.html](https://rulith.ai/brain.html).

**A long image conversation restored from the SSD cache no longer fails an allocation (#33)**
- After a long conversation with an image came back from the SSD cache, the next time the engine sized its buffers it read that conversation's image state and planned attention inputs over the whole KV pool - one of them alone 8,590,983,168 bytes at 524,288 cells, the allocation in the report.
- The engine now sizes its buffers independently of restored conversations, sizes a resumed batch by the cells its own conversation uses, and puts a restored conversation into one run of cells when the free space is scattered.
- Our reproduction: a 134K-token conversation with an image, saved to the SSD cache, the engine restarted, the conversation continued. 0.7.0 stopped with an error on the continuation; 0.8.0 answered, with 134,323 of its 138,450 prompt tokens from the cache and attention inputs sized to the conversation (138,496 cells instead of 524,288).
- "Keep conversations on disk" can be turned back on.
- Thanks to @guotianao for the report and the control run.

**Status checks no longer produce failed-login lines (#32)**
- The app checks the engine every few seconds. When it had adopted an engine whose key it did not know, those checks were sent without a key and the engine logged a rejected request for each one. The app now skips a server it cannot authenticate to and uses the current key of the engine it started, also after the key was changed in the settings.
- Thanks to @Aleprex for the report.

**Only one engine listens on a port (#28)**
- On Windows two engines could listen on the same address and port at once, so a status request could be answered by an older engine than the one whose log was being read - a slot that looked busy before any task.
- The engine now takes its port for itself: a second engine on the same address and port stops at startup with "couldn't bind HTTP server socket". In our check 0.7.0 let the second engine take the port; 0.8.0 refused it at once, the first engine kept answering, and stopping and starting the engine five times in a row on the same port worked every time.
- Thanks to @biolau for the report.

**For people who build the engine themselves**
